## 0. Verdict

The site's copy is competent and it is written from the wrong side of the desk. Almost every sentence has Inflexions as its subject: we design, we deploy, we embed, we deliver. The buyer appears as "your business" at the end of the sentence, if at all. Every competitor in the local set writes the same way, which is why the category sounds like one company with four websites.

The fix is not louder claims. It is a change of subject. The buyer's situation opens each beat, the proof answers the objection they are already carrying, and Inflexions appears as the answer rather than the announcer. The two variants below both do this. They differ in temperature, not in structure.

**Recommendation:** ship **Variant A (Precision)** on the home page. It is closer to how a CTO talks and it is the safer read for a first premium site in a conservative category. Hold **Variant B (Momentum)** for campaigns, the Academy, and the Careers side door, where warmth pays. Section 11 gives the reasoning.

## 1. The offer

### 1.1 What is being sold

Integration. The buyer can get a network from Cisco's channel, a cloud from Microsoft's, security from a Sophos reseller, and a data team from a consultancy. What they cannot buy from any of them is the guarantee that the four will work as one system, and that one accountable party will answer when they do not. That is the product. Every line of copy serves that sentence.

### 1.2 What the page asks for

One action, stated identically at the top and the close: **book a 30-minute architecture review.** Confirmed by the owner on 21 September 2026, including the written follow-up.

Its conversion properties, in the buyer's terms:

| Fear | The line that removes it |
|---|---|
| "This will eat my afternoon." | Thirty minutes. |
| "I will be sold to." | With a Solutions Architect, not a salesperson. No pitch. |
| "I will get nothing for my time." | You leave with a written view of what to fix first. |
| "This will cost me." | No cost, no obligation. Stated once, at the close, never in capitals. |

### 1.3 The engagement models behind the offer (the pricing frame's raw material)

From the services pages and the FAQ, all approved facts:

- **Professional Services**: fixed-scope projects. Two-week discovery, then a milestone plan before any work begins. Implementation typically 4 to 12 weeks. Thirty-day hypercare after go-live.
- **Managed Services**: monthly retainer. SLA tiers set to the client's risk tolerance. 24/7 monitoring, monthly performance reports, a dedicated account manager.
- **Support Services**: tiered support (L1 to L3), remote and on-site, SLA-tracked.
- **Vendor-neutral**: "we recommend what works, not what pays us the highest margin" (FAQ, verbatim). No lock-in.

There are no published prices, and there should not be. The pricing frame in Section 8 sells the *shape* of cost (fixed, predictable, exit-able), which is what an enterprise buyer actually needs before a conversation.

## 4. The tone competitors use

Fetched 21 September 2026, hero lines verbatim.

| Competitor | Hero line | Subject of the sentence | Proof in the hero |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inlaks | "We are Africa's leading Information Technology Systems Integrator." / "Powered by Innovation." | Inlaks | None. A superlative. |
| CWG Plc | "We Are Positioning Africa to Maximize the Future." | CWG | None. An abstraction. |
| Verge Nexus | "The infrastructure your enterprise will rely on tomorrow, architected today." / "Six disciplines. One accountable partner." | The infrastructure, then Verge | None, but the structure is closest to ours. Note the resemblance to "Four Pillars. Zero Gaps." |
| IPMC Ghana | "Customer Based Digital Solutions for Your Company." | Solutions | None. Generic. |

Three things every one of them does, and we will not:
1. **The company is the subject.** "We are", "we have", "we deliver". Our sentences open with the buyer's situation or a fact, and Inflexions enters as the answer.
2. **Adjectives instead of evidence.** Leading, immense, superior, innovative. We use a year, a number, a client, or a mechanism.
3. **The future tense as a promise.** "Will rely on tomorrow", "maximize the future". We write in the present and the past tense, because the proof already happened.

One competitor, Verge Nexus, is polished enough to be mistaken for us at a glance. The separator is proof: they have a four-stage engagement model; we have a national LTE core and two Tier III data centres. Lead with what they cannot say.

## 5. The two variants

Both obey the brand voice (authoritative, direct, precise, energetic, grounded) and the writing rules (active voice, second person for the client, first person plural for Inflexions, em dash as the momentum mark, British spelling, title case for headings, no empty superlatives).

| | **Variant A — Precision** | **Variant B — Momentum** |
|---|---|---|
| Archetype lead | Sage. The engineer who has done this before and does not need to raise their voice. | Magician. The partner who makes the turning point happen and says so. |
| Sentence shape | Short declaratives. Facts first. Fragments allowed. | Second person, forward motion, one em dash per beat. |
| Temperature | Cool. Reads like a well-run data hall. | Warm. Reads like the first meeting with a partner who gets it. |
| Risk | Can feel austere if the design is also austere. The 3D environment supplies the drama, so the copy can afford restraint. | Can tip into promise if a line lacks a fact behind it. Every B line here has one. |
| Best for | Home page, pillar pages, proposals | Campaigns, Academy, Careers, social |

## 11. Recommendation and how to decide

Ship **A** on the home page. Three reasons:

1. **The environment is already the drama.** A dark, breathing, sixteen-thousand-node object supplies all the momentum the page needs. Copy that also pushes reads as trying. Copy that stays still reads as certain.
2. **The buyer's register is A.** CTOs and IT directors evaluating for a tender write in fragments and numbers. Meeting them there says "we are your kind of people" without saying it.
3. **A is harder to imitate.** Any competitor can adopt warmth. None of them can adopt "Trusted with national infrastructure." (the Beat 3 line, approved 25 September 2026) because they do not have the projects.

Use **B** where the reader is choosing to be moved: the Academy pages, the Careers page, LinkedIn, and any campaign landing page. B's Careers line ("Build the thing the country runs on") is better than A's and should be used regardless of the home page choice.

If the owner wants evidence rather than judgement: run A as the control and B's hero headline and close as the only variables, with the review-request rate as the metric, once GA4 is live and there are at least 400 sessions per arm. Do not test the proof beats; they are facts, and facts are not variables.
